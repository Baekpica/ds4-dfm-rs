# The video VAE decoder's block GEMMs on BF16 operands: the `bf16i` arm (2026-10-29)

`vae-decoder-attribution-2026-10-29.md` section 8.3 left one lever in the FRAMES
phase: the decoder's four block GEMMs run in cuBLASLt's FAST class, which on this
toolchain is the TF32-class kernel `cutlass_..._s1688gemm`, and `tests/h3/vae_gemm_bm`
priced BF16 operands at 3.10x that. This unit builds that arm, prices it twice on the
Spark, and reports what the operator is deciding.

Commits `284c864b` (the arm) and `701597d5` (its outer GEMMs and its load rounding),
plus the one carrying this record. Spark `gx10-e1d2`, sm_121a, CUDA
13.0, no clock policy (sampled `clocks.sm` 2,405-2,411 MHz across the renders). Job A as
everywhere in this campaign: 768x768, 107 frames, seed 42, four evaluations,
`H3_DIT_SOL=0.9`, `H3_VAE_ATTENTION=tensor`, `H3_DIT_GEMM=fp8` (the tensorwise route),
the NVFP4 encoder; the arm's own renders add `H3_VAE_GEMM=bf16i`, and the baseline
keeps the campaign's `H3_VAE_GEMM=bf16`.

## 1. What the arm is

`h3d_vae_linear_bf16` is the call `h3d_vae_linear` makes, with `CUDA_R_16BF` for
input, weight and output. The F32-output form is not available: sm_89
returns `CUBLAS_STATUS_INTERNAL_ERROR` and sm_121 segfaults inside the call (that
unit's `~/f32out.txt`, `~/f32out2.txt`), which is why the arm has to be BF16 on both
sides and drags the block's consumers with it - except that this arm keeps them: it
narrows the activation, computes, and widens the result, so every consumer still reads
F32 (`vae_video::project`).

Three consequences, all named:

- The four block weights are loaded already rounded (`Store::load_bf16`) and their F32
  copies are never made, so the arm holds 4.83 GB of them instead of 9.65 GB.
- Each projection costs two extra passes over its own data: the activation narrowed
  (F32 read, BF16 write) and the result widened (BF16 read, F32 write). At job A's
  tile that is 623 MB a block, 1.21 TB over the phase in 4.71 s - 257 GB/s, inside the
  250-270 GB/s band every elementwise kernel in this phase holds, so the casts are
  already at their floor and only a BF16 consumer can remove them.
- The arm's numeric footprint is the GEMM's own: `tests/h3/vae_gemm_bm` reports
  `rel_rms 3.63e-3` for the arm and for the GEMM alone, identical to six figures -
  narrowing the activation and widening the result add nothing the BF16 operand and
  output rounding does not already carry.

The mode reaches the decoder through the environment, which is how the decoder has
always taken `H3_VAE_*`: `Decoder::load` creates its own context (`Gpu::create`), and
`h3d_create` reads `H3_VAE_GEMM` once. The host learns the context's own choice with
`h3d_vae_gemm_mode` rather than parsing the variable a second time, and the F32 route
stays the default.

`bf16i` is the one value of `H3_VAE_GEMM` whose first letter is not its key, so the
environment parser matches it whole and `h3d_set_vae_option("gemm", ...)` takes it as a
fourth word beside fp32, tf32 and bf16.

## 2. The first A/B, and what it gave back

One binary, `H3_VAE_GEMM=bf16` against `bf16i`, interleaved (r1 fast, r1 bf16i, r2
fast, r2 bf16i), plus one nsys capture per arm (`~/bf16i-*`).

| rep | `H3_VAE_GEMM=bf16` | `=bf16i` | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 29.646 | 26.406 | -3.240 |
| 2 | 30.185 | 26.073 | -4.112 |
| the two captures, r1 binary | 30.513 | 26.310 | -4.203 |

The captures say the arm's GPU window fell by 5.91 s (29.222 against 23.315), so 1.7 s
of the gain was not in the window at all. `misc/decn/decn-split.py` bounds the window
with the two kernels only the decoder launches, and the CLI's FRAMES line wraps
`vae_video::decode` - which loads the decoder itself. The arm's load is where the
difference sat: narrowing 9.65 GB of weights on one host thread. A workstation harness
of the same arithmetic measures 0.517 s a 2.42 GB piece (2.1 s for the block weights),
and the arm's own load, at 14.5 GB of traffic in 1.70 s, is 8.5 GB/s - the figure the
loop shape gives, not a memory limit.

Two fixes, both numeric-neutral, both in `701597d5`:

- `h3d_vae_linear` read the arm's mode as neither TF32 nor BF16 and fell through to
  the reference's F32 SIMT path for the three projections *around* the blocks
  (post_quant, x_embedder, proj_out): 2.713 ms a tile against 0.073, and
  `magma_sgemmEx_kernel` 143.81 ms in the arm's capture against 3.90 + 0.23 ms in the
  F32 route's. Their operands are F32 in both arms, so they now keep the FAST class the
  F32 route gives them; only the four block GEMMs are the arm's.
- The narrowing is split over the machine's cores (16 at most), the way
  `minimax_vq::release` and its siblings count workers. The map is independent per
  value and the ranges are contiguous, so no bit of the result can move;
  `vae_store::tests::narrowing_is_bit_identical_across_ranges` holds one range against
  2, 3, 8, 64 and one range per value.

## 4. The numeric contract

`tests/h3/vae_gemm_bm 2532 3` at the decoder's own shapes and job A's row count
(`~/bf16i2-bench.txt`):

| mode | ms a block | its deviation from the F32 path |
|---|---:|---|
| `fp32` | 88.981 | the reference |
| `tf32` | 12.478 | `max_abs 0.0445, rms 0.00437, rel_rms 2.75e-5` |
| `bf16` (the campaign's route) | 11.242 | identical to `tf32`, bit for bit |
| BF16 operands, GEMM only | 3.655 | `max_abs 1.413, rms 0.5768, rel_rms 3.63e-3` |
| **the arm** (narrow, GEMM, widen) | **6.512** | the same figures as the GEMM alone |

In situ, the two arms' frames - 107 frames, 189,333,504 bytes of 8-bit RGB:

| pair | bytes differing | max abs difference | per-frame rms |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `bf16` against `bf16i`, rep 1 | 10,006,987 (5.285%) | 6 of 255 | mean 0.2298, worst 0.2474 (frame 22) |
| `bf16` against `bf16i`, rep 2 | 10,006,987 (5.285%) | 6 of 255 | identical |
| the F32 route against itself | 0 | 0 | 0 |
| the arm against itself | 0 | 0 | 0 |

- **The mux pair**: PSNR `y 44.580 u 48.005 v 49.263`, average 45.554; SSIM
  `All 0.984659`. Lower than the frames' own figure because a different frame makes a
  different H.264 encode, and the comparison then carries the codec's own difference.
- **The frames' own figure**: PSNR average 60.56, min 59.97 over the sequence, which is
  the decoder's deviation and nothing else.
- **Determinism**: both arms reproduce their own MP4 and their own 107 PPM frames byte
  for byte across the two reps; the F32 route's digest is
  `4611c9478c00ee28...` in both reps in both cycles, the digest the whole campaign has
  been comparing, so the two commits moved nothing on that path.
- **What it does not measure**: a `rel_rms` of the four projection *outputs* on the
  real latent. Nothing in the port dumps them, and the arm's in-situ contract here is
  the 8-bit frames and the bench's own deviation table. A per-tensor dump would be a
  new debug switch, not this unit.

## 5. What the operator is deciding

Five to six seconds of a ~85 s render (-6.4%), and 19.3% of FRAMES, for BF16 rounding
on every decoder activation. For scale, within this campaign: the DiT's released FP8
route measures `rel 3.79e-2` and the tensor-attention arm `rel 1e-5` at a 50.28 dB
render; this arm's frames sit at 60.56 dB with no byte off by more than 6 of 255 and
5.3% of them off by at least one. **Correction, 2026-10-29 (see
[vae-readback-2026-10-29.md](vae-readback-2026-10-29.md) section 5): the decoder's
default is not `bf16`, it is the reference's F32 route (`H3D_VAE_GEMM_FP32`), while
every FRAMES figure in this record was measured with `H3_VAE_GEMM` set.** The two
arms' 30.2 against 24.4 s above are the same measurement they always were; what was
missing is the baseline they are 5.9 s apart *on*, which is 110.4 s on the same
job - so the choice between them is a ~80 s one, not a 5.9 s one. `bf16` (the F32
operands' FAST class) remains the arm this record recommends adopting; the
environment word is still one word, on the same binary.

## 6. What this does not establish

- **Quality beyond one four-step render.** At four evaluations the sampler turns a
  quantization-level perturbation into a different sample, but the decoder is *after*
  the sampler and gets a bit-identical latent from both arms, so the pair above does
  measure the decoder's own deviation. It says nothing about a converged sampler or
  about how the deviation would look at another geometry.
- **That the arm is the end of the lever.** The casts are 4.71 s at the streaming
  floor, and they exist only because the consumers read F32. The full-BF16 chain the
  arm's own section 3 of the handoff describes (BF16 twins of rms_norm, video qkv rope,
  the attention, swiglu, scale_add and the layer norm) would remove them and halve the
  elementwise traffic too - of the order of another 4.8 s - at the price of six new
  kernels and a much wider numerical surface. That is the next unit's decision, not
  this one's.
- **That the phase is now near its floor.** 2.97 s of host-side gaps between tiles,
  1.87 s of device-to-host readback and 1.60 s of rope kernel sit in the same phase and
  none of them is addressed here.
- **Anything about the audio VAE, the DiT, or another geometry.** One shape, one tile
  plan, two captures.
- **That the host's own options can reach the decoder.** `LoadOptions::vae_gemm` is
  applied to the DiT's context (`dit.rs`), and the decoder builds its own; only the
  environment configures it today. That is how the campaign has always run, and it is
  why `bf16i` is an environment word rather than a request field.
