# The per-row FP8 arm, built and priced (2026-10-29)

Unit: the reference's `granularity="channel"` route as a third arm of
`H3_DIT_GEMM`, after [`fp8-row-scales-2026-10-29.md`](fp8-row-scales-2026-10-29.md)
found that cuBLASLt will not apply the two scale vectors itself. That record
priced the epilogue at "about 27 ms at this host's ~250 GB/s ... under 1% of an
8.0 s evaluation"; that estimate is 50x low, because it summed the four
matrices once instead of once per block. Section 8 corrects it, and section 5
is the measured price. The verdict the operator needs first: **the arm is
exact and reproducible, costs 1.73 s more than the tensorwise arm per
evaluation to keep 47% of the FP8 win, and the render does not show a quality
gain that pays for it** - the release's own tensorwise route stays the one to
run.

Commit: the one landing this record. Spark `gx10-e1d2`,
sm_121a, CUDA 13.0,
`/data`-side reference; job A as everywhere in this campaign (768x768, 107
frames, seed 42, four evaluations, `H3_DIT_SOL=0.9`, the NVFP4 encoder).

## 1. What was built

Three kernels and one epilogue beside the tensorwise triple
(`native/h3/h3_dit_gpu.cu`, entry points at `h3_dit_gpu.h`):

- `h3d_fp8_scale_rows` - the row amax (`h3d_fp8_amax_rows_kernel`, one block per
  row, no atomics: the row is the reduction's own unit) and the row scale;
- `h3d_fp8_quantize_rows` - the tensorwise kernel's rounding with the divisor
  taken from the element's own row;
- `h3d_linear_e4m3_raw` - the E4M3 matmul with no scale attributes at all and
  an **F32** output;
- `h3d_fp8_scale_epilogue` - `out[i][j] = bf16(acc[i][j] * row[i] * col[j])`.

`h3d_linear_e4m3` and `h3d_linear_e4m3_raw` are now the two calls of one static
`h3d_linear_e4m3_compute`; the tensorwise arm's descriptor, layouts, TRANSB,
alpha and beta are unchanged, and its attributes are set only when the caller
passes scales. `H3_DIT_GEMM=bf16|fp8|fp8-row` is read once per load
(`dit.rs`, `gemm_mode_from_env`); `MatrixScale` carries either the tensorwise
F32 or the per-row vector, so the four call sites of `projection` are unchanged.

## 2. The reference's arithmetic, read again at the pin

`fastvideo/layers/quantization/fp8_config.py` at `3d8ac9d1` (the revision
`configs/dependencies.json` pins), re-read this unit:

- activation, `_quantize_rowwise` (`:63-68`): `x_absmax = x_2d.abs().amax(dim=-1,
  keepdim=True).float()`, then `(x_2d / x_scale.to(x_2d.dtype)).clamp(-448,
  448).to(float8_e4m3fn)` - one scale per **token**;
- weight, `convert_model_to_fp8`'s channel branch (`:226-229`):
  `w_absmax = weight_local.detach().abs().amax(dim=1).nan_to_num().float()`,
  then `(weight_local / w_scale.to(weight_local.dtype).unsqueeze(1))...` - one
  scale per **output channel**, and the divisor is the scale rounded to BF16,
  exactly as the tensorwise kernel already does;
- matmul (`:147-153`): `torch._scaled_mm(x_fp8, w_fp8.t(), scale_a=x_scale,
  scale_b=w_scale.view(1, -1), out_dtype=torch.bfloat16)`. The vectors go
  **into** the matmul, so they multiply the F32 accumulator before the BF16
  cast: one rounding, not two.

The port mirrors the last point: the raw matmul leaves F32 and the epilogue
rounds once.

## 3. The reproducer's evidence

`tests/h3/fp8_gemm_bm <rows> <k> <n> 11` (`mode 11`) at the four DiT projection
shapes, 18,799 rows. Four checks per shape; every one of them is exact:

| shape | rows x k x n | row quantization | epilogue vs accumulator | all-ones identity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| qkv | 18799 x 5376 x 21504 | 0 of 101,063,424 A and 0 of 115,605,504 W bytes off | 0 of 404,253,696 differ | 0 of 404,253,696 differ |
| fc1 | 18799 x 5376 x 28672 | 0 of 101,063,424 and 0 of 154,140,672 | 0 of 539,004,928 | 0 of 539,004,928 |
| out | 18799 x 7168 x 5376 | 0 of 134,751,232 and 0 of 38,535,168 | 0 of 101,063,424 | 0 of 101,063,424 |
| fc2 | 18799 x 14336 x 5376 | 0 of 269,502,464 and 0 of 77,070,336 | 0 of 101,063,424 | 0 of 101,063,424 |

Read them as four separate statements:

- **The two per-row reductions are the host's, not merely close to it.** The
  check recomputes each row's amax and scale on the host (`check_quantized_rows`)
  and compares the device's own vector element by element: worst scale relative
  error 0 at all four shapes, and every E4M3 byte within half an ulp of the
  host quotient. So the one-block-per-row reduction and the per-element divisor
  lookup are right, not just plausible.
- **The epilogue is exact, not approximate.** The check reads the F32
  accumulator back and recomputes `bf16(acc * row * col)` on the host as two
  `fmaf(x, y, 0)` products - the host's way of writing a single rounded
  multiply - and finds **0 of 404,253,696 (and 539,004,928) elements
  different**. The kernel's arithmetic is therefore exactly two F32 multiplies
  then one BF16 rounding, which is the reference's expression.
- **The all-ones identity holds bit for bit.** With unit vectors the unscaled
  matmul plus the epilogue equals `h3d_linear_e4m3` with unit scales, at all
  four shapes: 0 elements differ out of 404,253,696 (and 539,004,928). This is
  what re-checks the refactor of the shared cuBLASLt helper: the scaled call
  and the raw call produce the same accumulator.
- **The arm's own quantization noise, against the tensorwise arm's.** Same
  operands, same seed, one process each: tensorwise `RMS 0.941476` (rel
  `0.0379`), per-row `RMS 0.935681` (rel `0.0376`) at qkv; fc1 `0.0376`, out
  `0.0371`, fc2 `0.0373` for the per-row arm.

That last line is the arm's whole value proposition, and it is unimpressive
**on these operands**: 0.0376 against 0.0379 is a 0.8% smaller error. The
reproducer's synthetic operand places a 6x outlier every 4,096 elements, so
every 5,376-wide row contains one: the tensorwise scale is already the
per-row scale of the unlucky rows. A real weight's outliers concentrate in a
few output channels, where per-row scaling helps much more - which section 6
cannot confirm, and indeed finds it cannot measure.

## 4. The per-call prices

Median of three timed repetitions, first discarded, GPU empty, 18,799 rows
(`mode 11`'s own report). The quantize row covers **both** the activation and
the weight pair, so only the activation half belongs to an evaluation - the
weight is quantized once at load; section 7's nsys table is the per-evaluation
number.

| shape | quantize rows | raw F32 matmul | epilogue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| qkv 18799x5376x21504 | 4.842 ms | 24.612 ms | 11.786 ms |
| fc1 18799x5376x28672 | 5.732 ms | 32.687 ms | 15.714 ms |
| out 18799x7168x5376 | 3.870 ms | 7.953 ms | 2.743 ms |
| fc2 18799x14336x5376 | 7.952 ms | 15.435 ms | 2.714 ms |

Two readings:

- **The epilogue is bandwidth-optimal and is the whole price.** qkv's reads
  1.617 GB of F32 and writes 0.808 GB of BF16 in 11.786 ms: 206 GB/s, against
  this host's ~235-250 GB/s streaming rate. It cannot be made cheaper; it can
  only be removed (section 8).
- **Writing F32 instead of BF16 costs the matmul.** `fp8-row-scales`'s mode 9
  measured the same unscaled matmul at 21.588 ms for qkv **with a BF16
  output** (a different session); the F32 output the epilogue needs makes it
  24.612 ms, the extra 0.808 GB of store.

### 4.1 The three calls, one process

The two arms' nsys tables are differenced captures from different sessions, so
`mode 13` runs the three calls the two arms make on the same operands in one
process: the release's scaled matmul with its BF16 output, the raw matmul the
per-row arm needs, and that matmul plus the epilogue. Four repetitions, first
discarded, medians.

| shape | scaled, BF16 out | raw, F32 out | raw + epilogue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| qkv 18799x5376x21504 | 21.443 ms | 25.024 ms | 36.819 ms |
| fc1 18799x5376x28672 | 28.783 ms | 32.963 ms | 48.879 ms |
| out 18799x7168x5376 | 7.030 ms | 8.131 ms | 10.733 ms |
| fc2 18799x14336x5376 | 14.735 ms | 15.458 ms | 17.769 ms |
| **per evaluation** (x 50 blocks) | **3,600 ms** | **4,079 ms** | **5,710 ms** |

So the GEMM's own share of the per-row arm's price is **+479 ms/eval (+13.3%)**
for the F32 output, and the epilogue is **+1,631 ms/eval** on top. Those two
plus the activation quantization are the whole difference between the arms.

## 5. The three arms, end to end

Job A, one binary, three arms interleaved over two rounds, the canonical
environment (`H3_DIT_SOL=0.9 H3_VAE_ATTENTION=tensor H3_VAE_GEMM=bf16`, the
NVFP4 encoder), `--steps 4`, head of each round the same prompt and seed:

| arm | round 1 | round 2 | MP4 sha256 (both rounds) |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| bf16 | 44.230 s | 44.746 s | `5b5e735c1ebcbf2e...` |
| fp8 tensorwise | 31.372 s | 31.739 s | `4611c9478c00ee28...` |
| fp8 per-row | 38.428 s | 38.508 s | `c44a341081e2f9de...` |

Mean `DENOISE` and what it is against the BF16 arm: tensorwise 31.556 s,
**-12.932 s, -29.1%**; per-row 38.468 s, **-6.020 s, -13.5%**. Per evaluation
(four of them): 11.122 s, 7.889 s, 9.617 s. The per-row arm therefore gives
back **1.728 s per evaluation, +21.9%, against the tensorwise arm already
landed**, and it keeps only 47% of the FP8 win.

Two cross-checks in the table, both of which it had to pass:

- **Each arm reproduces its own bytes across the two rounds**, and the BF16 and
  tensorwise digests are the *previous* unit's own
  (`fp8-row-scales-2026-10-29.md` section 3, same hashes). That is what
  re-checks the refactor of `h3d_linear_e4m3` into its shared helper: the
  scaled arm's outputs are unchanged to the byte, so the extraction moved no
  arithmetic.
- **`LOAD` is not the per-row arm's cost.** Its per-row weight amax and
  quantize run at load instead of the tensorwise pair: LOAD 9.519 and 10.270 s
  for the per-row arm against 8.401/8.779 (bf16) and 10.433/9.542 (tensorwise)
  at the same page-cache state - inside the spread this phase shows run to run.

## 6. Quality

`rowmetrics.sh` on the six renders: ffmpeg's own PSNR/SSIM against the BF16
arm, and the campaign's detail energy (whole-render mean of
|`luma - gblur(luma)`|, the instrument `fp8-row-scales` section 4 names, run at
its scale) on each render on its own. The BF16 and tensorwise renders are
byte-identical across the two rounds, so every number below is exact for its
arm, and the same-arm pairs are `inf` dB / SSIM 1.000000.

| render | detail sigma 4 | detail sigma 8 | PSNR y vs BF16 | SSIM Y vs BF16 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| bf16 | 1.8839 | 2.9788 | - | - |
| fp8 tensorwise | 1.4284 | 2.4477 | 21.720 dB | 0.715096 |
| fp8 per-row | 1.5709 | 2.5932 | 20.924 dB | 0.698148 |

The two readings disagree, and looking at the frames says why: **the three arms
are three different samples of the same shot, not three renderings of one
sample.** Same prompt, same seed, same schedule, but the four-step sampler is
not converged, so a quantization-level perturbation moves the realization.
Frame 90 of each arm, the fox's head, cropped 384x384 from (384, 192):

- BF16 - head down, muzzle left, ears back;
- tensorwise - head up, ears forward, muzzle right;
- per-row - head lower and further forward, a third pose.

And it is not a phase shift of the same motion. Correlating BF16's frame 90
against the tensorwise arm's frames 85..95 and the per-row arm's, the PSNR is
18.93 / 17.87 dB at offset 0 and stays between 17.1 and 19.0 dB across the whole
window - flat, with no peak at any offset.

That makes both metrics unusable as *quality* here, and the numbers say so on
their own: the two FP8 arms differ from **each other** by 21.172 dB (SSIM Y
0.757590) while differing from BF16 by 21.720 and 20.924 dB - the *smallest*
perturbation of the three pairs, `rel 0.0379` against `rel 0.0376`, i.e. a
0.8% difference in quantization error, produces the same render distance as the
full 0.0379. The distance has saturated: it measures how far a four-step
sampler carries a perturbation, not how large the error is. PSNR and SSIM
against BF16 rank arms by how much they *diverged*; the detail energy counts
whatever high-frequency content the particular pose and noise realization
carry, which is why `fp8-row-scales` section 4's own visual check already had to
withdraw a softness claim.

So the honest statement of this unit's quality result: **the finer granularity
does not show up as a better image, and the instrument could not have shown it
either way.** The decision belongs to section 5's price and to fidelity with the
release's own route, both of which point at the tensorwise arm. The
methodological consequence is larger than the arm: ranking DiT arms by
end-to-end four-step renders does not work; it needs a converged sampler or a
teacher-forced metric.

Note what this does *not* do to the tensorwise arm's case, since it is the arm
this campaign runs: its quality is the release's own contract rather than
something these renders establish (the release ships W8A8 FP8 on the DiT's
projections, `runtime/stage1.py:126-134`), and the 21.7 dB between it and BF16
is a sample distance, not a measured loss.

- **The projection block is the whole difference.** Tensorwise: 3,803 (GEMM) +
  1,114 (activation quantization) = 4,917. Per-row: 4,106 + 709 + **1,662
  (epilogue)** = 6,477. Delta **+1,560 ms**, against the wall difference of
  +1,728 ms/eval from section 5 (an nsys sum of positive per-kernel deltas is a
  lower bound on the wall). Every other kernel is unchanged within ~10 ms,
  which is the cross-check that the arm moves one thing.
- **The epilogue is exactly where section 4 put it**: 1,662 ms/eval measured by
  nsys against the bench's 1,648, 343 GB at 206 GB/s.
- **Both GEMM shapes are slower with the F32 output**, by 9% and 5%, and both
  run under a different kernel name (`qqsss` against the scaled arm's `qqtst`):
  the unscaled call does not merely reuse the scaled kernel without its
  scales, it selects a different one.
- **The two methods bracket the GEMM's share.** nsys, which measures each arm's
  own kernels in its own session, says +303 ms/eval; section 4.1's same-process
  trio says +479. The wall difference (section 5) is the number to use, and at
  +1,728 ms/eval it sits inside the sum of those pieces either way: the pieces
  are +303..479 (GEMM) and +1,631..1,662 (epilogue), less whatever the per-row
  activation reduction saves. Do not read the decomposition as exact; a 15%
  spread between two methods is what these captures support.
- **The per-row reduction looked 2.2x faster than the tensorwise one on the
  same bytes** (281 ms/eval against 611 for 200 calls each). That would have
  been a free lever on the arm the campaign actually ships, so it was checked
  in one process (section 7.1): it does not hold. Both reductions run at
  ~213 GB/s, i.e. the streaming rate, and the row arm's nsys figure is the one
  that matches the controlled rate - the tensorwise table's amax row is 2.15x
  it and should be re-measured before it is quoted again.

## 8. The correction to `fp8-row-scales` section 6, and the option it leaves

That record estimated the epilogue at "about 27 ms ... under 1% of an 8.0 s
evaluation". It is **1,648 ms per evaluation** - 61x more - and the error is
arithmetic, not measurement: it summed the four output matrices once when an
evaluation runs each of them once per block, 50 times. The per-call figure it
would have given (32.96 ms for the four at 18,799 rows) is right; the
per-evaluation one forgot the blocks. Section 4's per-call column and this
unit's nsys table are what to read instead, and the predecessor now carries the
correction.

The one route that would make per-row granularity affordable is to **remove the
epilogue pass rather than speed it up**: the four consumers already read the
projection's output (attention's `qkv_rope` reads `qkv`, `swiglu` reads `fc1`,
the gate and the residual reads read `out` and `fc2`), so each could take the
F32 accumulator and apply `row[i] * col[j]` on load, at no extra traffic. The
price is paid once, on the store: the matmul would write F32 instead of BF16,
which the four projections priced at 231 ms per evaluation together.

It is deliberately **not** pursued, for two reasons that belong in the record:

1. it is a deviation from the reference. The reference's `_scaled_mm` rounds
   the scaled accumulator to BF16 (`out_dtype=torch.bfloat16`) and its
   consumers read that BF16; folding the scales into the consumers would have
   them read the unrounded F32 and round at their own output instead. That is a
   different rounding point, i.e. a numerical change, to be named and measured
   against the reference rather than slipped in while claiming the arm is the
   same arithmetic;
2. it would buy a granularity whose benefit this unit could not demonstrate
   (section 6) in exchange for four kernel changes across the attention, MLP
   and residual paths.

## 9. What this does not establish

- **Which arm has the better image.** Section 6's two metrics disagree and
  neither is decisive; the campaign has no reference image to measure either
  arm against, and the release's own product is a different geometry
  (1344x768 out of an H3 draft at 672x384x124), so its render cannot be the
  reference here.
- **The 231 ms/eval the F32 store costs.** It is the difference between this
  bench's raw matmul (4,034 ms/eval at the four shapes) and the tensorwise
  arm's nsys-projected GEMM (3,803 ms/eval) - a cross-method pairing. Section
  7's table is the arm's own numbers under one method.
- **Whether a *block* granularity would be affordable.** The outer-vector
  mode's `NOT_SUPPORTED` (`fp8-row-scales` section 5) says nothing about
  `CUBLASLT_MATMUL_MATRIX_SCALE_VEC32_128x128` and friends, which belong to the
  block-scaled MX/NVFP4 family this port does not run; untested here.
- **That the disagreement in section 6 is a property of the arms** rather than
  of this one shot. One geometry, one prompt, one seed, four evaluations.
